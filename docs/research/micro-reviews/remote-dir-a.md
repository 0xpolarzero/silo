## REMOTE-DIR-A-1: Mutex contention lets queued work start after its deadline

- **Priority:** P2.
- **Location:** `app/SiloUI/src-tauri/src/remote/operations.rs:175` (clock sampled before locking), `:180` (deadline comparison), `:153` and `:227` (journal writes under the same mutex).
- **Trigger:** A queued remote change reaches its gate admission check shortly before its deadline. Another submission or completion holds the registry mutex while writing and syncing its journal marker. `wanted()` samples `Instant::now()` before waiting for that mutex; the write stalls until after the queued change's deadline. Its connection and management permission remain open.
- **Evidence:** `wanted()` obtains `now` at line 175, locks at line 176, and compares that saved timestamp against `operation.deadline` at line 180. `accept()` holds this mutex across `write_marker()` at line 153; `finish()` also holds it across the write at line 227. `write_marker()` performs both file and directory `sync_all()` at lines 338–342. There is no duration bound on either sync. The operation gate invokes this predicate immediately before admission (`runtime/operation_gate.rs:703–723`) and marks the condition started after a true result; it has no independent remote-deadline check. Thus a check begun before the deadline can return true and admit work after it. This is a source-confirmed interleaving, not an executed timing reproduction.
- **Consequence:** A change whose start budget elapsed can still mutate the owner after waiting for unrelated journal I/O. This breaks the registry's stated contract that an unstarted change past its deadline never starts, including when the controller is still connected and waiting for an expiry result.
- **Suggested fix:** Sample the admission clock after acquiring the registry mutex, immediately before checking the deadline. Keep the existing gate and journal; no new subsystem is needed.
- **Test that would catch it:** Queue a change behind a held VM gate. In a deterministic registry test, hold the registry mutex while releasing the VM gate, let the admission predicate reach the mutex wait before its deadline, advance an injected clock past the deadline, and release the mutex. Assert the result is `EXPIRED` and the mutation counter stays zero. Use a barrier or a narrow clock seam to establish the order rather than relying on disk speed or sleeps.
