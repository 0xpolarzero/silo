# Creative review

## First animatic: do not expand

The initial proposal, “a room inside your other computer,” has a useful product
invariant: the VM and its work remain on the owning computer. The first staging
does not express that invariant through its geometry. The guest screen floats
above the hardware from the beginning, so the hardware acts as a pedestal.

The dominant progression is a wide view of two computers, a scale into an
editor/browser pair, a small edit, and a scale back out. The visible light-to-dark
change is clear, but the composition mostly advertises the example website.
The stage does not yet show why Silo makes the work possible or accessible.

Decision: preserve as rough diagnostic evidence; no full-film expansion,
soundtrack production or detail polish. Ask for two different ways to stage
the core consequence, including one that challenges the section-cut concept.

## Criteria for the next motion proof

- The VM stays identifiable as work inside an owning computer.
- Access from the controlling computer becomes legible through movement.
- At least one transition changes how the product is understood, rather than
  merely bringing another window into view.
- The frame has a clear visual subject at phone playback size.
- The viewer sees a consequence, not just an instruction and a pointer.
- Coherent timing includes deliberate contrast; constant easing and repeated
  scale changes do not establish a rhythm on their own.

Rough assets and incomplete surface polish are acceptable at this stage.
Clipped typography, exact icon geometry and final audio levels are later checks.
They do not determine whether the organizing idea deserves a full film.

## Second and third proofs

The first independent-workspaces test made the benefit clearer, but held two
upright panels in nearly the same places for about six seconds. Its dropdown
and export steps extended a tutorial after the useful simultaneous action.
Decision: retain the idea; reject that staging for full-film expansion.

B2 changes the whole frame: begin inside one human workspace; the Silo VM row
opens a dominant second space; human drag and agent maximize occur together;
the workspace edges then resolve into two physical computers. The action is
short and the guest result stays inside its owner. The primary agent inspected
the frame sequence, sought encoded transition frames in the local reviewer,
and checked the central result at 390-pixel playback width.

Decision: accept this organizing structure for production. This does not
approve its rough surfaces, hardware proportions, approximate mark or cursor
geometry as final artwork. The production film must integrate SSH and local
port access through the same VM and owner, rather than append feature chapters.

## Full production draft review

Primary opened the encoded 29.8667-second draft in the local browser reviewer,
played it and sampled workspace, ownership, terminal, network and closing
frames. Review also included source stills at full resolution and a 390-pixel
playback view. This is browser playback with sampled visual observations, not
a claim of uninterrupted audiovisual perception or a listening review.

The structure is accepted. Bounded corrections requested before final encode:

- Preserve the previous workspace beneath terminal opening.
- Keep type proportions during the physical-computer reveal.
- Tie the initial Start cursor to the actual action center.
- Explicitly give the pale VM row a dark foreground inside Terminal.
- Use one port label during the address handoff and match its final baseline.
- Remove the new three-pixel border at the guest surface's frame-352 handoff.
- Increase the terminal command and Network endpoint hierarchy for small views.
- Include the editor action beside Terminal without adding a vendor sequence.

A low-resolution adjacent-frame difference scan found its largest changes
during the intended workspace opening. It is a diagnostic for suspect seams,
not proof that every animation is smooth. The final corrected encode still
needs its own targeted visual and media checks.

## Delivery rejected

The user rejected the delivered film. Primary's previous creative acceptance
was wrong. Sampled frames, portions of browser playback and technical media
checks were substituted for a continuous end-to-end audiovisual review and
the requested creative standard.

Reinspection of the delivered contact sheet and source shows the core failure:
the film is dominated by generic Notes/report interfaces and repeated panel
resizing. The geometric hardware remains rough. The terminal-to-Network
transition exposes white masking rectangles over terminal text around 16.5s.
These are not reasons to describe the delivery as ready with minor nitpicks.
This version is retained as rejected development evidence. Earlier archived
versions are untouched. No further creative rewrite is started by this review.
